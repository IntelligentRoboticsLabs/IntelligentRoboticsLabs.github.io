About the project

Human-robot interaction keeps growing, and so do the events involving robots. Besides safety and security, robots need explainability systems that let us understand what happened and why, to keep trusting them and to make them accountable. DMARCE investigates how to meet these requirements with state-of-the-art robot software frameworks.

Following the EU guidelines for trustworthy AI, the project addresses four requirements: accountability, transparency, privacy, and technical robustness and safety. The result is the design, development and evaluation of software that provides explainability to autonomous robots, translating robot behaviour into human language while taking the cybersecurity dimension into account.

Subprojects

EDMAR · Universidad de León

Explainability in the Decision Making for Autonomous Robots

A log-based auditing framework that generates accountability reports for developers and explanations of robot behaviour for the general public.

CASCAR · URJC

Cybersecure And Safe Cognitive Architectures for Robots

Detection and mitigation of threats in robot programming frameworks and cognitive architectures, tamper-evident evidence for later explanation, and safety mechanisms when a cybersecurity problem occurs.

Demos/Experiments

  • 2023 Demo 0: RIPS scenario, no attacks. Robocup's receptionists.
  • 2023 Demo 1: In this experiment, we model a scenario wherein an unauthorized entity gains access to the data stream containing video footage captured by the robot's front-facing camera. RIPS detects the anomalous behavior and disables the front camera.
  • 2023 Demo 2: RIPS detects an unauthorized use of the navigation service and forbids the navigation within a restricted zone.
  • 2023 Demo 3: RIPS is monitoring the topics of the system and inspecting the messages. It uses a YARA rule to detect a signature of the malicious payload. To mitigate the attack, RIPS activates a System Mode to halt the robot.
